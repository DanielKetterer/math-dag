1 :  Wrong as a strict prerequisite: Shapes in different categories can share attributes without first mastering the general concept of defining attributes; direct comparison of examples supplies a credible route.

4 :  Wrong as a strict prerequisite: Telling time to the nearest five minutes can be taught using the minute hand’s five-minute marks and the hour hand’s position; telling time to the half hour is a useful scaffold, not indispensable prior knowledge.

5 :  Wrong: Measurement word problems involving simple fractions do not necessarily involve subtraction; the proposed prerequisite belongs to subtraction-specific problem types, not the general application node.

7 :  Too broad if unconditional: Multiplying a one-digit number by tens or hundreds supports the partial-quotients route to division, but it is not necessary for every credible division strategy; retain it within the appropriate route if that route is explicitly represented.

10 :  Too broad if unconditional: Multi-step unit-conversion problems need not involve fractional measurements; keep fractional-measure conversion only for the relevant problem type or alternative route.

11 :  Wrong: Parallel lines can be defined and understood without first defining a line segment; the relevant prerequisite is the concept of a line extending indefinitely in both directions, not the segment between endpoints.

14 :  Overstated: Defining a rhombus requires understanding the four-sided category and equal-side condition, but the stronger skill of recognizing quadrilaterals of every shape is not strictly necessary before learning that definition.

17 :  Too broad if unconditional: Counting forward by ones is one route to totaling coins when pennies are included, but other collections can be totaled using skip-counting, known coin values, or addition; treat this as a route-specific dependency.

22 :  Wrong as a strict prerequisite: Representing the distributive property with an area model can introduce the grouping of \\(b+c\\) directly; prior mastery of parentheses as an order-of-operations rule is not indispensable, and parentheses do not require evaluating the grouped expression before distributing.

25 :  Overstated: Learners can establish that squares are quadrilaterals by counting four sides without first mastering the full definition of a square, including equality of sides and right angles.

28 :  Too strong: Decimal notation for thousandths can be introduced through place-value structure, powers of ten, or fractional representations; mastery of the hundredths node is a natural teaching route, but not a logically indispensable prerequisite.

29 :  Wrong as a strict prerequisite: The definition of a square can be learned directly through four equal sides and four right angles; general recognition of arbitrary quadrilaterals need not precede it.

30 :  Wrong as a strict prerequisite: A learner can read an analog clock to the nearest minute by counting individual minute marks; nearest-five-minute reading is a useful shortcut and typical progression, not a necessary prior skill.

67 :  Do not require the full volume concept first: Counting unit cubes is itself a credible route to developing the idea that volume is the number of unit cubes that fill a solid; use a related link or split concrete measurement from the more formal conceptual node.

72 :  No edge to a purely numerical midpoint node: Showing half-hour time involves positioning the minute hand halfway around a clock face; a number-line midpoint skill is not strictly required unless that node explicitly includes spatial or circular midpoint reasoning.

73 :  Yes, conditionally: Keep subtraction within 100 as one route for bar-graph comparison problems whose scale allows differences beyond 10; if the assessed range is limited to 10, the broader subtraction node is unnecessary.
